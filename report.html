1. Data wrangling

Inspect both missing and present values with isna() and notna(). Trim text whitespace and convert empty strings to missing values before repeating the audit. Parse dates with an explicit format and coerce invalid dates/numbers to missing so errors are visible.

Missing demographic labels or dates should be checked against the source, then quarantined if they cannot be recovered. Missing sales or units should not be filled with zero or an overall average: either choice could distort revenue and rankings. Recover them from a verified source or exclude and disclose affected records. Negative amounts need investigation because returns could be legitimate. Duplicate rows need source verification before removal; identical aggregates are not automatically errors.

Cleaning and coverage decisions

No values need imputation if the audits above report no missing or invalid entries. Whitespace is removed, and all valid records are retained. Absent calendar dates are a coverage limitation, not evidence of zero sales; do not manufacture sales for those dates. The listed daily row counts help assess whether all 7 × 4 × 3 = 84 combinations appear on observed days. A constant Sales/Unit ratio means revenue and unit rankings contain the same information; it is not evidence that this price applies outside this dataset.

Min–max normalization

Use (value − minimum) / (maximum − minimum) to put Unit and Sales on a 0–1 scale. This preserves their relative ordering and is easy to interpret. It is sensitive to extremes, so inspect the box plots before using it for modeling. Keep original values for financial reports; summing normalized values does not produce revenue. If modeling later, fit normalization limits on training data only.

GroupBy recommendation

Use groupby() to split records into state, demographic, and time chunks and then aggregate sales and units. It summarizes existing rows; it does not merge separate datasets. Use merge() with validated keys if branch counts, population, or campaign costs become available. Revenue alone cannot tell whether a state has poor productivity or simply fewer stores.

def period_report(freq):
    temp = df.assign(Period=df.Date.dt.to_period(freq))
    report = temp.groupby('Period').agg(Sales=('Sales','sum'), Units=('Unit','sum'),
        Records=('Sales','size'), Observed_days=('Date','nunique'))
    report['Calendar_days_in_Q4'] = [len(pd.date_range(max(p.start_time,pd.Timestamp('2020-10-01')),
        min(p.end_time.normalize(),pd.Timestamp('2020-12-31')))) for p in report.index]
    report['Missing_days'] = report.Calendar_days_in_Q4 - report.Observed_days
    return report
daily = df.groupby('Date')[['Sales','Unit']].sum()
weekly = period_report('W-SUN')
monthly = period_report('M')
quarterly = period_report('Q-DEC')

Business interpretation

  • Expansion: VIC is the strongest revenue market in the supplied quarter. Investigate branch capacity and profitability there before committing expansion funds. Revenue is not profit, and branch counts are unavailable.
  • Lower-revenue markets: Prioritize small controlled pilots in WA, NT, and TAS. Test locally targeted bundles, loyalty offers, and inventory availability. Compare each pilot with similar untreated stores or customers; measure incremental revenue, conversion, and gross margin after discounts. Low total revenue alone does not establish low demand per store.
  • Demographics: Men lead and Seniors are lowest, but totals are close. Test relevant products and accessible offers for Seniors rather than assuming a large demographic performance gap. Customer counts and conversion data are needed to explain the difference.
  • Time of day: Morning is the peak and Evening is the off-peak period by total sales. The difference is small, so avoid major staffing changes based on this ranking alone. Test an evening offer and measure incremental margin; the dataset has time labels rather than exact hours and contains no personal customer information for individual targeting.
  • Planning: Compare monthly totals with observed-day counts. A single Q4 cannot establish annual seasonality, sustained growth, or a forecast for next year. Missing calendar days and partial weekly periods must remain visible in comparisons.

Limitations and next steps

This file has no store counts, costs, customer IDs, campaign exposure, or prior-year comparison. Obtain those fields and clarify absent dates and currency before major investment decisions. The constant sales-to-unit ratio makes revenue and volume rankings identical. These are descriptive findings, not causal proof that a campaign or state characteristic caused sales.
